* A Personal Policy for General LLM Use (In Progress)

This text presents high level information of my personal use of LLMs with space to fill in with examples, anecdotes, processes later.

This document is primarily serving to keep myself sane in the midst of changes in our industry. I need a knife to cut through the noise, a horn to blast clearly over the hype, a guide to keep my work and personal experience centered on what matters, no matter how quickly the technology around changes.

---

* The Goal of Our Work

At the advent of LLMs, which can perform previously unsuspecting types of work at increasing speed and with increasing effectiveness, the goal of our work, all work, remains the same. I believe this bears repeating because the onset of this technology is sweeping through many sectors like a storm. The goal of our work remains the same. It is to improve the world in some way. It is to build the earth and protect it, for each other.

As a software engineer, I split this goal into three things. Our job is to: get stuff done, improve the craft, and enjoy the process. This is very generic, on purpose. Because the hard part, perhaps the fulfilling part, of our job is to know what stuff should be done, to know what our craft is and what it means to improve upon it, and to learn how to enjoy each step of the muddy way.


1. Get Stuff Done (Fulfilling Others)

- Does it work?
- Is it useful?
- Can it be better?

What does “better” mean? Many lean toward the most obvious in our industry, they say “make better by making it faster”. This could be an improvement for sure. But it’s not the only vector that can be applied in this regard. “Better” could also mean: more reliable, clearer, optimized, safer.


2. Improve the Craft (Fulfilling the Work)

And what is our craft? Is it coding? Is it gathering domain knowledge? Or is it more general? Is it problem solving? Is it making business requirements real? Wherever we land, we can say there’s enough involved in all the real-world things we make happen to say there’s space to specialize and a need to define, refine, and teach fundamentals.


3. Enjoy the Process (Fulfilling Yourself)

If we don’t enjoy our work, or at least the outcome of our work, can we sustain it at all? Can we continue to get stuff done well and improve upon the craft itself, if we are burnt out from the mechanics of the process?

* Rules to LLM By

- Act first, prompt later. Act = think, envision, create, write, edit
- You are responsible, not the LLM.
- Use the LLM to expand the problem space. Reduce the problem space yourself.
- Use the LLM to complete work you can verify yourself.
- LLMs are generative. Add as little as necessary to solve your problem.
- LLMs are not subtractive. Subtract as much as possible from your solution.
- Use LLMs like a crow. Use it to build the tool that can repeat the work you would have just asked it to do ex nihilo.
- There is always another person at the end of your work.

---

* Questions to Ask Oneself When Using LLMs

1. The Personal
- Am I passing off work that would require reasoning or problem solving?
- Where does the meaning come from in this work?

2. The Primary

- What work will I skip over right now by using the LLM? 
- What would I gain if I performed this work myself? Or why is it not worth doing myself?
- What is the cost of getting this work wrong, or implementing it poorly?

3. The Practical

- Could a tool be built to do what I'm asking AI to do? If yes, build the tool. If no, answer why not.
- What are the first hand sources from which the LLM came to its conclusions?
- How would I verify the output myself if I had to?
